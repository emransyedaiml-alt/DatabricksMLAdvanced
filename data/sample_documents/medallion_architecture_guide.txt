Medallion Architecture Guide: Bronze, Silver and Gold

Our lakehouse organises data in three layers.

Bronze layer
Bronze stores raw data exactly as received from the source, plus ingestion metadata such as file name and
ingestion timestamp. No business rules are applied. Bronze lets us replay history if a downstream rule changes.

Silver layer
Silver holds cleaned and conformed data. In Bronze-to-Silver processing we apply data types, deduplicate records,
enforce data quality rules, standardise codes (for example currency and country), and join reference data.
Silver tables are the trusted source for analytics and data science.

Gold layer
Gold contains business-level aggregates and data products, such as daily sales by region or customer lifetime
value, shaped for reporting tools and dashboards.

Difference between Bronze and Silver processing
Bronze processing focuses on landing data reliably and completely, without changing it. Silver processing focuses
on correctness: types, duplicates, quality rules and consistent keys. A failure in Bronze usually means the data did
not arrive; a failure in Silver usually means the data arrived but broke a rule or a schema expectation.
